There is a moment every practitioner knows. You did the technique. You imagined the scene, night after night. And the thing did not happen. It is the moment most people quietly abandon the practice — usually with the conclusion that either the law is false, or something is wrong with them.

What almost nobody knows is that Neville wrote about this moment *directly*. Not in passing, not defensively — he gave failure its own chapter, near the end of [The Power of Awareness](/books/the-power-of-awareness), and opened it with the exact question you are asking:

> "It is entirely possible that you either have had or will have a number of failures in this respect – many of them in really important matters. If... you faithfully apply it in an effort to attain some intense desire and fail, what is the reason?"
> — [The Power of Awareness, Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24)

So let's do what this site does: answer the question from the sources. Here is Neville's own troubleshooting guide — his two named causes of failure first, then the specific failure modes he corrects across the rest of the corpus.

## The two causes Neville names

In Chapter 24, Neville reduces every failure to two roots: **you did not persist**, or — the subtler one — **it never felt natural**.

The first is self-explanatory, and he had already been blunt about it: "an Assumption, though false, *if persisted in* will harden into fact" ([Chapter 4](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-4)). Three nights is not persistence. Persistence ends when the assumption feels like a memory, not when the calendar says so.

The second cause is the one he calls "the most important factor in the successful use of the law":

> "The time it takes your Assumption to become fact... is directly proportionate to the naturalness of your feeling of already being what you want to be... The fact that it does not feel natural to you to be what you imagine yourself to be is the secret of your failure."
> — [The Power of Awareness, Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24)

Read that again, because it quietly reframes everything: the technique did not fail — it simply never finished. An assumption that still feels like pretending is a seed that never germinated. And Neville immediately gives the remedy: naturalness "can be achieved by persistently filling your consciousness with imagination — imagining yourself being what you want to be or having what you desire." Not by force. By occupancy.

## The specific failure modes, source by source

Under those two roots, the corpus corrects a handful of precise mistakes. Check yourself against each one honestly.

### 1. You watched the movie instead of being in it

The most common technical error, and Neville's own test for it is the ladder: visualize yourself climbing a ladder, then close your eyes and *feel yourself actually climbing*. The first is a picture; the second is an experience. "The difference between FEELING yourself in action, here and now, and visualizing yourself in action, as though you were on a motion-picture screen, is the difference between success and failure" ([Five Lessons, Lesson 1](/books/five-lessons/five-lessons-lesson-1)). If you appear *in* your scene, you are still the audience. Step behind your own eyes — the [SATS guide](/techniques/sats) walks through this.

### 2. Your scene wandered

From the same lesson: "Experience has taught me to restrict the imaginary action... to condense the idea into a single act, and to re-enact it over and over again until it has the feeling of reality." A different scene every night, or a five-act epic that drifts into associations, plants nothing. One short scene. The same one. Until it feels like memory.

### 3. You fell asleep in the old state

The nightly threshold matters more than any session: "Never go to sleep feeling discouraged or dissatisfied. Never sleep in the consciousness of failure" ([Feeling is the Secret, Chapter 2](/books/feeling-is-the-secret/feeling-is-the-secret-chapter-2)). Ten minutes of imagining followed by thirty minutes of worried rehearsal before sleep is a diet of worry with a garnish of practice.

### 4. You argued against it all day

Whatever the nightly scene plants, the daytime inner conversations water — or poison. Neville's warning is surgical: "If you find the world unchanged, it is a sure sign that you are wanting in fidelity to the new mental diet, which you neglect in order to condemn your environment" ([Awakened Imagination, Chapter 7](/books/awakened-imagination/awakened-imagination-chapter-7)). An hour of assumption cannot outvote fifteen hours of inner arguing. The [Mental Diet guide](/techniques/mental-diet) is the daytime half of the practice.

### 5. You kept digging up the seed

Asked what to do between the assumption and its fulfillment, Neville answered: nothing. "It is a delusion that, other than assuming the feeling of the wish fulfilled, you can do anything to aid the realization of your desire" ([The Power of Awareness, Chapter 21](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-21)). The morning evidence-audit — scanning phone, inbox and world for signs — is not neutral. Every check re-affirms *not yet*. [Letting go](/techniques/letting-go) is part of the technique, not an afterthought.

### 6. It never became natural — the master key

All the modes above are really this one wearing different costumes. The state you return to by default is the state that out-pictures. If "I am that" still feels like a costume, Neville's counsel is not to push harder in the moment but to *revisit more often*, until familiarity does what force cannot. Naturalness is a threshold, and repetition — calm, felt, first-person — is how you cross it.

## What failure does not mean

This may be the most valuable paragraph in the chapter. Neville anticipates exactly the conclusion you are tempted to draw, and forbids it:

> "You must not believe that because your Assumption failed to materialize, the truth that assumptions do materialize is a lie. If your assumptions are not fulfilled, it is because of some error or weakness in your consciousness. However, these errors and weaknesses can be overcome."
> — [The Power of Awareness, Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24)

Failure, in his frame, is data — not a verdict on the law, and not a verdict on you. An experiment with an error in it is corrected and re-run, not mourned.

## Restart tonight

Pick the failure mode that stung the most as you read it — that is almost certainly yours. Then rebuild the practice around it: one short scene, entered in first person, in the drowsy state, ended in the feeling of accomplishment, guarded by the daytime diet, and released.

And if the real casualty was your *confidence* in the law itself, don't restart with the desire that hurts most. Rebuild the foundation first with the [Test the Law experiments](/test-the-law) — seven low-stakes proofs, starting with the same ladder Neville used on his skeptics — and come back to the big desire with your own evidence in hand.

Then read [Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24) in full. It is short, it is direct, and it was written for precisely the evening you are having.
