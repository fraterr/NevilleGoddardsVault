Every practitioner knows this moment. You did the technique. You imagined the scene, night after night. The thing did not happen. Most people quietly give up right here, and they usually walk away with one of two conclusions: the law is false, or something is wrong with them.

Very few people know that Neville addressed this moment directly. He gave failure its own chapter near the end of [The Power of Awareness](/books/the-power-of-awareness), and he opened it with the same question you are asking:

> "It is entirely possible that you either have had or will have a number of failures in this respect – many of them in really important matters. If... you faithfully apply it in an effort to attain some intense desire and fail, what is the reason?"
> — [The Power of Awareness, Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24)

So let's answer the question from the sources. This is his own troubleshooting guide: the two causes he names in that chapter, followed by the specific mistakes he corrects elsewhere in the corpus.

## The two causes Neville names

In Chapter 24, Neville reduces every failure to two roots. Either you did not persist, or the assumption never came to feel natural.

The first one needs little explanation. He had already been blunt about it in [Chapter 4](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-4): "an Assumption, though false, *if persisted in* will harden into fact". Three nights is not persistence. Persistence ends when the assumption feels like a memory. It does not end when the calendar says so.

The second cause is the one he calls "the most important factor in the successful use of the law":

> "The time it takes your Assumption to become fact... is directly proportionate to the naturalness of your feeling of already being what you want to be... The fact that it does not feel natural to you to be what you imagine yourself to be is the secret of your failure."
> — [The Power of Awareness, Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24)

This reframes the whole experience of failure. The technique did not fail. It never finished. An assumption that still feels like pretending is a seed that never germinated. Neville gives the remedy in the same chapter: naturalness "can be achieved by persistently filling your consciousness with imagination". You do not force the new state. You occupy it until it stops feeling foreign.

## The specific mistakes

Under those two roots, the corpus corrects a handful of precise errors. Check yourself against each one honestly.

### 1. You watched the movie instead of being in it

This is the most frequent technical error, and Neville had a test for it: visualize yourself climbing a ladder, then close your eyes and feel yourself actually climbing one. The first is a picture. The second is an experience. "The difference between FEELING yourself in action, here and now, and visualizing yourself in action, as though you were on a motion-picture screen, is the difference between success and failure" ([Five Lessons, Lesson 1](/books/five-lessons/five-lessons-lesson-1)). If you can see yourself in your scene, you are still in the audience. Step behind your own eyes. The [SATS guide](/techniques/sats) walks through how.

### 2. Your scene wandered

From the same lesson: "Experience has taught me to restrict the imaginary action... to condense the idea into a single act, and to re-enact it over and over again until it has the feeling of reality." A different scene every night plants nothing, and so does a long epic that drifts off into associations. One short scene. The same one. Repeat it until it feels like something that happened.

### 3. You fell asleep in the old state

What you carry across the threshold of sleep matters more than any session. "Never go to sleep feeling discouraged or dissatisfied. Never sleep in the consciousness of failure" ([Feeling is the Secret, Chapter 2](/books/feeling-is-the-secret/feeling-is-the-secret-chapter-2)). Ten minutes of imagining followed by thirty minutes of worrying in the dark is a diet of worry with a garnish of practice.

### 4. You argued against it all day

The nightly scene plants something, and your daytime inner conversations either water it or poison it. Neville is direct about this: "If you find the world unchanged, it is a sure sign that you are wanting in fidelity to the new mental diet, which you neglect in order to condemn your environment" ([Awakened Imagination, Chapter 7](/books/awakened-imagination/awakened-imagination-chapter-7)). An hour of assumption cannot outvote fifteen hours of inner arguing. The [Mental Diet guide](/techniques/mental-diet) covers the daytime half of the practice.

### 5. You kept digging up the seed

Asked what to do between the assumption and its fulfillment, Neville answered: nothing. "It is a delusion that, other than assuming the feeling of the wish fulfilled, you can do anything to aid the realization of your desire" ([The Power of Awareness, Chapter 21](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-21)). The morning ritual of scanning your phone, your inbox and the world for signs is not harmless. Every check tells you it has not happened yet. [Letting go](/techniques/letting-go) belongs to the technique itself, not to some optional appendix.

### 6. It never became natural

The mistakes above are all versions of this one. The state you return to by default is the state that gets expressed in your life. If "I am that" still feels like a costume, the answer is not to strain harder in the moment. Revisit the state more often, in the calm way described above, and let familiarity do what force cannot. Naturalness is a threshold. Repetition carries you across it.

## What failure does not mean

This part of the chapter deserves the most attention. Neville anticipates the exact conclusion you are tempted to draw, and he rules it out:

> "You must not believe that because your Assumption failed to materialize, the truth that assumptions do materialize is a lie. If your assumptions are not fulfilled, it is because of some error or weakness in your consciousness. However, these errors and weaknesses can be overcome."
> — [The Power of Awareness, Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24)

In his frame, a failure is information. It says nothing about the law and nothing final about you. You correct the error and run the experiment again.

## Restarting tonight

As you read the list above, one of the mistakes probably stung more than the others. Start there, because it is almost certainly yours. Rebuild the practice around it: one short scene, entered in first person, in the drowsy state, closed with the feeling of accomplishment, protected by your daytime conversations, and then left alone.

If what actually broke was your confidence in the law itself, do not restart with the desire that hurts most. Rebuild your foundation on smaller targets first with the [Test the Law experiments](/test-the-law), then return to the big desire carrying your own evidence.

And read [Chapter 24](/books/the-power-of-awareness/the-power-of-awareness-chapter-24) in full. It is short, it is direct, and it was written for the evening you are having.
